EUOperational resilience

DORA: keeping financial services running

Applicable

DORA has applied since 17 January 2025. It brings together requirements for technology risk, ICT incidents, resilience testing and ICT suppliers for financial entities within its scope.

A practical next step

Link each relevant requirement to an owner, a control and evidence. Start with your ICT risk assessment, incident process, test results and supplier register.

Check your scope

Check the entity types, exclusions and proportionality provisions. A technology supplier and a regulated payment institution do not have identical duties.

  • Central Bank of Ireland: DORA ↗
  • Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 ↗

EUFinancial crime

AML/CFT and AMLA: who sets the rules?

Current duties and transition

AMLA is the EU authority for anti-money laundering and countering terrorist financing. Existing duties continue; the new AML Regulation generally applies from 10 July 2027, while AMLA's direct supervision of selected entities begins in 2028.

A practical next step

Review customer due diligence, beneficial ownership, transaction monitoring and escalation. Record which new requirements affect your business and who will implement them.

Check your scope

AMLA will not directly supervise every fintech. AMLR has sector exceptions; the new AML Directive has phased national transposition dates.

Official sources

  • AMLR: Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 ↗
  • AMLA: role and timeline ↗
  • AMLD6: Directive (EU) 2024/1640 ↗

EUPrivacy and data

GDPR: understand and protect personal data

Applicable

GDPR governs personal-data processing within its scope. It can also cover businesses outside the EU when they offer goods or services to people in the EU or monitor their behaviour there.

A practical next step

Map your data, purpose, lawful basis, access and retention. Keep evidence of privacy information, processor arrangements, security and breach handling; assess when an impact assessment is required.

Check your scope

Assess establishment and targeting, rather than nationality alone. UK data-protection requirements need a separate assessment.

Official sources

  • Irish Data Protection Commission: GDPR scope ↗
  • European Commission: GDPR applicability ↗
  • GDPR: Regulation (EU) 2016/679 ↗

EUPayments

PSD3 and PSR: prepare for payments reform

Awaiting Parliament's second reading

The payments reform package has an agreed negotiated text, but both official Parliament dossiers still await second reading on 18 November 2026. Prepare for changes without presenting PSD3 or PSR as already applicable replacement rules.

A practical next step

Maintain a change log for authorisation, safeguarding, fraud prevention and customer protection. Recheck the final law and its application dates before committing a delivery deadline.

Check your scope

Relevant to payment and e-money business models. Continue to assess current PSD2 and national requirements during the legislative transition.

Official sources

  • European Parliament: PSD3 procedure ↗
  • European Parliament: PSR procedure ↗
  • Council: political agreement on payments reform ↗

EUAI governance

EU AI Act: classify the use, then plan the controls

Phased application

The AI Act applies in phases and was amended by the AI Omnibus in July 2026. The amended high-risk timetable is 2 December 2027 for Annex III systems and 2 August 2028 for systems linked to Annex I regulated products; other provisions already apply.

A practical next step

Keep an AI inventory with intended use, accountable owner and legal classification. Assess relevant transparency, oversight and testing duties, and support staff AI literacy with evidence of practical measures.

Check your scope

Purpose, role and specific provisions matter. An internal low, moderate or high risk rating is not a legal classification; a workshop does not certify compliance.

Official sources

  • European Commission: AI Act ↗
  • European Commission: AI Omnibus in force ↗
  • AI Omnibus: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 ↗

EUCrypto-assets

MiCA: when the product involves crypto-assets

Applicable; assess crypto scope

MiCA regulates crypto-asset activities within its scope. National transitional arrangements for existing crypto-asset service providers have ended by November 2026; the EU-wide maximum transition ended on 1 July 2026.

A practical next step

Classify the asset and service before choosing an authorisation route. Check the provider's permission, customer-asset arrangements, complaints, disclosures and operational controls.

Check your scope

This topic is relevant where crypto-asset or stablecoin exposure exists. Some already regulated financial entities can use an Article 60 notification route for specified services.

Official sources

  • Central Bank of Ireland: MiCAR ↗
  • ESMA: Article 143 transitional measures ↗

UKPayments

UK payments: safeguarding customer funds

Applicable

UK payment and e-money firms have their own safeguarding regime. The FCA's Supplementary Regime took effect on 7 May 2026, strengthening records, oversight, reporting and other protections for relevant customer funds.

A practical next step

Identify relevant funds, accountable oversight and reconciliation controls. Review reporting, audit applicability and resolution information against the current FCA rules.

Check your scope

Check the firm's permission and activity. The UK framework is separate from EU PSD3/PSR; requirements and exemptions vary by institution.
