Example engagements

What a Lippi engagement could look like.

Illustrative scenarios: these hypothetical examples explain potential work and deliverables. They are not client engagements or achieved results.

Illustrative · payments business

Making risk ownership and reporting clear

The challenge: policies and spreadsheets exist, but control ownership is unclear, actions remain open and leadership receives inconsistent risk reports.

How I could help: review risk and control records, agree owners, prioritise gaps and establish a regular action-review and reporting routine.

Potential deliverables: a consolidated risk and control register, responsibility map, 90-day remediation plan and management dashboard.

Intended benefit: leadership can see who owns each issue, what needs a decision and which actions are overdue.

Illustrative · technology supplier

Preparing for a financial customer’s due diligence

The challenge: a software provider receives detailed questions about resilience, suppliers and incidents, while its evidence is scattered across teams.

How I could help: clarify the provider’s role and customer requirements, map existing evidence and coordinate gap closure with technology and security specialists.

Potential deliverables: a requirements-to-evidence map, named control owners, a remediation tracker and documented incident escalation arrangements.

Intended benefit: more consistent customer responses and a clear view of the work still needed. The engagement would not assume that every DORA obligation applies directly to the supplier.

Illustrative · fintech adopting AI

Putting practical governance around AI use

The challenge: teams use several AI tools without a shared inventory, consistent approval process or clear ownership of the risks.

How I could help: map AI use cases, assess relevant roles and requirements, and establish proportionate governance with business, technology and privacy stakeholders.

Potential deliverables: an AI inventory with owners, approval and oversight workflows, supplier review actions and a prioritised governance plan.

Intended benefit: leadership can understand where AI is used, who is responsible and which decisions need further review.

Where Lippi comes in.

Are you essentially a fractional Chief Risk Officer?

Fractional risk leadership is one option, alongside defined reviews and implementation projects. I can coordinate priorities, provide challenge, track actions and prepare leadership reporting. A formal regulated CRO appointment is a separate engagement, subject to the responsibilities and approvals required for your business.

How do you compare with training and certification providers?

Lippi’s main role is helping your team implement and manage the agreed work: procedures, control owners, evidence, remediation and reporting. Workshops can support that implementation. Specialist assessors and independent auditors handle formal validation or assurance; Lippi can coordinate readiness with your chosen provider.

We already have a risk or compliance team. Why bring in Lippi?

You may need extra delivery capacity for a control improvement, remediation programme, supplier review or reporting change. I work alongside your team against agreed deliverables and reporting lines. Your leadership retains its decisions and accountability.
