The programme

Four workshops.
A clearer next step.

Follow a fictional payments business through each session, with examples of expansion in both directions between Brazil and Europe. Apply the lessons to your own questions through discussion, exercises and practical worksheets.

01 2 hours

Licensing & market choice

Where could we operate, and which permissions fit our activity?

  • Compare licensing routes in the European Union, the UK and Brazil.
  • Understand payment and e-money routes, with an introduction to other fintech activities.
  • Consider local management, capital, customer funds, preparation costs and dependencies.

Take away A market-comparison worksheet and questions to resolve before an application.

02 2 hours

GRC in practice

What must we put in place to run the business responsibly?

  • Connect governance, risk, controls and compliance.
  • Assign business ownership, oversight and independent assurance.
  • Work through outsourcing, cyber risk, incidents, reporting and capital planning.

Take away A starter risk-and-control worksheet and a prioritised action list.

03 2 hours

The main regulations

Which requirements matter to our business model?

  • Learn how to scope which rules in the European Union, the UK and Brazil may apply to your services.
  • Explore PSD2, PSD3/PSR developments, AML/KYC, GDPR and LGPD, DORA and outsourcing.
  • Introduce MiCA and the EU AI Act where relevant, including AI governance, ownership and oversight.

Take away A regulatory mapping worksheet and official sources for further work.

04 2 hours

RegTech, cyber & AI tools

How can tools support our controls and decisions?

  • Explore one practical GRC, cybersecurity and AI use case.
  • Connect demonstrations to a risk, control owner and evidence requirement.
  • Assess fit, integration, data handling, oversight and implementation effort.

Take away A tool-evaluation scorecard and a first implementation checklist.

An introductory programme built around payments examples. Individual workshops and a four-session programme are planned. Applicability depends on your activities and market. Detailed application work, technical assessments and company-specific advice are separately scoped.

A supplier fails.
Who owns the response?

A fictional fintech plans to expand its wallet service. A processor outage prevents customer payments. The board asks who will restore service and what evidence it should receive.

Fictional learning example

Start with the business decision.

What is the customer impact? Who is accountable for the service? What needs escalation? Which records will show that the response worked?

Who should normally own the operational response?
Own & act

Business and operations

Lead recovery, manage the supplier, assess customer impact and record actions and decisions.

Guide & challenge

Risk and compliance

Challenge the assessment, help identify applicable obligations and escalate gaps through governance.

Assure independently

Internal audit

Assess whether the framework and controls work. Management owns the corrective actions.

In the workshop: turn this scenario into a risk statement, a named control owner, an escalation trigger and evidence for the board.
